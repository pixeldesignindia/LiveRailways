Understand the distance.

← UK network map   UK route directory ↗

The first UK edition distinguishes reference railway distances from atlas estimates. Neither is a live measurement of the distance remaining on a train.

  • Published rail reference: a rounded railway distance from the linked reference. Several principal corridors use Network Rail Consulting’s 2014 distance table (page 7); only its distance figures are reused. Its old timetable times and operator names are not used. Other references are identified separately in each route’s Details.
  • Planning estimate: an editorial approximation for the named railway corridor, generally rounded to 5–50 km. These are broad planning figures, not outputs from a verified track-routing engine, operator mileage or surveyed chainage. Their uncertainty has not been measured. Actual paths, terminals, reversals and diversions can make the journey longer or shorter.
  • Operator journey figure: a distance advertised by the operator, retaining its stated basis. The Cardiff–Manchester figure is a rounded conversion of TfW’s “just over 170 miles”.

Corridors are different from station mileposts

Historical mileposts can follow an older route. For example, western station mileages measured via Bristol can exceed the distance of a modern direct train via Newbury and Castle Cary. The selected map connections illustrate a corridor and do not trace the tracks.

Times and operators

Journey times come from linked operator pages, a stated timetable example, or a named independent timetable source. The directory labels the basis in Details. A typical or average time is not a minimum. A sleeper’s overnight time is shown separately from daytime operators where both are listed. This is selected coverage, not an exhaustive timetable or operator inventory.

Long-distance scope

The UK map includes selected intercity and long-distance scenic corridors. City networks, Underground, metro, tram, suburban and commuter-only routes are excluded. Mainline tracks used by long-distance trains may also carry local services; those local services are not mapped as a separate network. Belfast–Dublin is a separate network on the island of Ireland, with Dublin in the Republic of Ireland.
