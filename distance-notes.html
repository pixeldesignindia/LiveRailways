Distance is an estimate unless identified otherwise.

Planning estimate means a rounded editorial approximation for the named rail corridor. It is not operator-published mileage, a verified track-routing result or surveyed chainage. Actual station choices, service routes, tunnels and diversions can change the distance. No measured error margin is available. Do not use these estimates to calculate a train’s kilometres remaining.

Published route reference means a separately sourced route length. The Glacier Express’s 291 km end-to-end reference is linked in its route details. It is still not a live measure or a promise about diversion mileage.

Times describe the source offering.

Each operator’s Details show whether a time is advertised, average, fastest or illustrative, and whether the source describes the reverse direction. An approximate sign does not turn an average into a minimum. Dates, stations, connection times and stopping patterns matter. Some records group multiple services; choose a dated itinerary with the linked operator before travelling.

Amsterdam is a city-level label: Eurocity Direct uses Amsterdam Zuid, while the cited Eurostar routes use Amsterdam Centraal. Check the exact departure station. International travel times exclude separate security and check-in procedures where required.

Selected mainlines, excluding local city networks.

These first editions cover selected intercity, international and long scenic mainline routes. Local metro, tram, S-Bahn/S-train and urban stop-by-stop networks are excluded. Short mainline intercity links are included because these countries are compact. The Swiss directory includes Glacier Express and Bernina Express scenic mainline journeys, subject to their seasonal timetables and reservations.

The maps place connection points geographically but simplify the links. They do not trace tracks, show every station or claim official operating zones. Colour groups belong to this independent atlas. Cross-border routes may appear in more than one country directory.

Italy: Sicily is a separate network on this map; mainland through trains require the Strait of Messina train ferry. No rail bridge or Sardinian sea connection is drawn. Ireland: Dublin represents both Heuston and Connolly, which require a city transfer if changing between them; Belfast is in Northern Ireland and there is no rail link to Great Britain. Denmark: Hamburg services use Jutland and Padborg, not the future Fehmarn Belt tunnel. Great Belt and Little Belt crossings use existing rail bridges.

Portugal: selected CP examples use Santa Apolónia or Oriente in Lisbon, Campanhã in Porto and Coimbra-B. Celta arrives at Vigo Guixar; elapsed durations account for the local-time difference with Spain. The Douro record is a connection example, not a fastest-time claim. Austria: ÖBB and WESTbahn may use different Vienna stations. The map includes the Koralmbahn and the German corridor between Salzburg and Tyrol. Poland: the directory combines general tourism references, operator timetable examples and explicitly identified independent timetable references; older announced or seasonal figures are labelled in Details. It is not an autumn 2026 fastest-journey ranking.

Hungary: Budapest station choices differ by train; Hungarian and Romanian local times differ by one hour. Romania: selected Astra Trans Carpatic distances reproduce its operator timetable kilometre references, with the source identified on each route. They are not surveyed chainage. Bulgaria: BDZ service notices supply scheduled endpoint examples, not delay-adjusted travel times. Sofia-Sever and Ruse-Razpredelitelna differ from the main passenger stations. International examples include connections and waits. BDZ publishes several date-specific autumn/winter timetables; the Istanbul example uses 2 November–12 December 2026 and accounts for Türkiye staying on UTC+3 while Bulgaria uses UTC+2. Halkalı is outside central Istanbul. Greece/Serbia passenger links are not included; seasonal services and work-related transfers must be checked with the operator.

Albania: no current long-distance passenger service could be verified. HSH lists undated Durrës Plazh–Peqin, Shkodër–Gjormë and Lushnjë–Rrogozhinë regional timetables. Dashed map links identify these references, not verified operating services. Tirana–Durrës is shown as a rehabilitation project. No invented durations or route distances are supplied. Contact HSH before travel.

Greece: selected mainline trains, including the separate Evros line. International passenger links and bus replacement sections are excluded. Athens/Larissa/Thessaloniki times use Interrail’s general indicative references; other selected examples use Hellenic Train PDFs linked in Details. A directory entry does not guarantee operation on a particular date.

Türkiye: the map extends across Anatolia. Istanbul Halkalı and Söğütlüçeşme are distinct stations, with separate records; a city transfer may be required. YHT durations use general operator or Ministry references, not a dated fastest-time ranking. İzmir Mavi and the regular Doğu Ekspresi are conventional overnight trains; the seasonal tourist Eastern Express is a separate, longer offering. Sofia sleeper uses the separately dated BDZ winter example.

Norway: Narvik belongs to the Swedish cross-border network and has no rail link to Bodø. Nordland night trains are currently suspended; the daytime Trondheim–Bodø service is listed. No railway to Tromsø is implied. Domestic sleeper alternatives are not all catalogued. Check current works with the operator.

Sweden: Stockholm–Kiruna may require a change at Boden. The Åre sleeper example is seasonal winter 2026/27. Snälltåget uses Gothenburg Gamlestaden/Mölndal and Copenhagen Syd; other services can use Central stations. Snälltåget Oslo trains do not run 29 October–1 November 2026. Routes and operators shown are selected offerings.

Finland: Oulu–Haparanda passenger services began 10 August 2026. Finland is one hour ahead of Sweden; the listed time is elapsed time. Change at Haparanda for Norrtåg services into Sweden. A through Helsinki–Stockholm train is not implied. Lapland destinations may need onward buses beyond the railhead; no rail links to Norway, Estonia or Russia are shown.

Times and operators were reviewed on 24 October 2026. This is selected coverage, not a complete or live timetable. Source links appear in each route’s Details.
